## 2. Repeatability of nominally identical evaluations

`stretchA` and `stretchB` are the same transform, the same checkpoint and the same 300
episodes, run independently. The configuration equivalence is proven at code and hash
level; the *execution-environment* equivalence (same driver build, no confounding load)
is corroborated by the operational timeline rather than instrumented per arm — see
`REPRODUCTION_CHECK.md` section 6. Source: `tables/repeatability_*.csv`.

**Paired outcome contingency table** (n = 300):

| | stretchB success | stretchB failure | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **stretchA success** | 30 | 15 | 45 |
| **stretchA failure** | 18 | 237 | 255 |
| **total** | 48 | 252 | 300 |

- stretchA SR = 15.00 % (45/300); stretchB SR = 16.00 % (48/300) — the aggregate rates agree closely.
- Raw paired agreement = 89.00 %; **paired discordance = 11.00 %** (33/300 episodes changed outcome between two identical runs).
- Cohen's kappa = **0.5802**, 95 % CI [0.4515, 0.7088] (asymptotic) and [0.4410, 0.6979] (episode bootstrap, 10 000 resamples).
- Positive agreement = 0.6452; negative agreement = 0.9349. The asymmetry matters: agreement on failure is high simply because failure is common, so kappa alone understates how unstable the *successes* are.
- Of the 63 episodes that succeeded in *either* replicate, only 30 (**47.6 %**) succeeded in *both*.
- Exact McNemar test on the 33 discordant pairs (15 vs 18): p = 0.7283 — no directional bias, as expected for two runs of the same configuration. The instability is symmetric, not a drift between arms.
- Scene-cluster bootstrap (8 scenes, 10 000 resamples): kappa 95 % CI [0.4937, 0.6610]; discordance 95 % CI [7.53, 14.67] %.

  Note that the scene-cluster interval for kappa is *narrower* than the episode-level
  bootstrap. This is expected rather than anomalous: cluster resampling preserves each
  scene's internal composition intact, and per-scene kappa is fairly homogeneous, so
  less variation is induced than by resampling episodes independently. Quote the
  episode-level or asymptotic interval as the primary CI for kappa and treat the
  scene-cluster one as a robustness check — do not present it as the conservative bound.

Wording note for the manuscript: describe this as **run-to-run outcome instability**
(irreproducibility of per-episode outcomes), not as "randomness". The data establish
that identical configurations yield different per-episode outcomes; they do not
characterise the process generating that variation.

Figure: `figures/repeatability_paired.pdf`

## 3. Differential wall-clock timeout censoring

Every episode is killed at a fixed **900 s wall-clock** budget (`EP_TIMEOUT=900` in `arms_runner.sh`, enforced by `timeout -k 30`) and recorded as a
failure with `term_reason=wall_timeout`, `success=0`, `spl=0`. This is a *compute*
limit, not a navigation outcome. It is distinct from the simulated-time episode cap
(`--max_episode_s 120`, i.e. 6000 sim steps), which produces `term_reason=step_cap`.
Source: `tables/censoring_summary.csv`, `censoring_bounds.csv`, `censoring_tests.csv`.

| arm | n | successes | timeouts | timeout % | ITT SR % | completed-episode SR % (sensitivity) | SR lower bound % | SR upper bound % | bound width (pp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stretchA | 300 | 45 | 20 | 6.67 | 15.00 | 16.07 | 15.00 | 21.67 | 6.67 |
| stretchB | 300 | 48 | 22 | 7.33 | 16.00 | 17.27 | 16.00 | 23.33 | 7.33 |
| crop | 300 | 50 | 6 | 2.00 | 16.67 | 17.01 | 16.67 | 18.67 | 2.00 |
| pad | 300 | 40 | 37 | 12.33 | 13.33 | 15.21 | 13.33 | 25.67 | 12.33 |
| h060 | 200 | 23 | 10 | 5.00 | 11.50 | 12.11 | 11.50 | 16.50 | 5.00 |
| h078 | 200 | 25 | 13 | 6.50 | 12.50 | 13.37 | 12.50 | 19.00 | 6.50 |
| h095 | 200 | 37 | 9 | 4.50 | 18.50 | 19.37 | 18.50 | 23.00 | 4.50 |
| h110 | 200 | 26 | 6 | 3.00 | 13.00 | 13.40 | 13.00 | 16.00 | 3.00 |
| h130 | 200 | 30 | 15 | 7.50 | 15.00 | 16.22 | 15.00 | 22.50 | 7.50 |
| h150 | 200 | 29 | 13 | 6.50 | 14.50 | 15.51 | 14.50 | 21.00 | 6.50 |

- The **intention-to-treat SR is the preferred estimate**. The completed-episode column
  is a *sensitivity analysis only*: dropping timed-out episodes is itself biased, since
  slow episodes are plausibly the harder ones, which is why every arm shifts upward.
- The **partial-identification bounds** are assumption-free over the censored episodes:
  the lower bound counts every timeout as a failure (and therefore equals the ITT SR by
  construction), the upper bound counts every timeout as a success. The true SR under
  an unlimited compute budget lies in that interval. `pad`'s interval is 12.33 pp wide versus 2.00 pp for `crop`.
- **Timeout rates are not equal across arms.** crop 6/300 vs pad 37/300: odds ratio 0.1451, Fisher exact p = 7.04e-07, risk difference -10.33 pp.
- Control: the same test between the two identical replicates (stretchA vs stretchB) gives OR 0.9026, p = 0.8731 (-0.67 pp) — the timeout mechanism itself is stable run-to-run, so the crop-vs-pad gap is a property of the arms, not of run-to-run noise.
- By contrast the crop-vs-pad **success-rate** difference is not significant: OR 1.3000, Fisher p = 0.3035, +3.33 pp.

**Differential-censoring decomposition** (descriptive, not causal):

```
d_ITT=+3.3333pp (=risk_diff_sr above)
d_completed=+1.7977pp
attenuation=(d_ITT-d_completed)/d_ITT=+0.460697 (+46.07%) = share of the observed ITT gap that does not survive restriction to completed episodes. NOT a causal claim: neither gap is statistically significant (ITT SR Fisher p=0.3035), and the completed-episode restriction is itself a biased comparison (drops plausibly-harder slow episodes differentially by arm's timeout rate).
```
This quantifies the share of the observed ITT gap that does not survive restriction to
completed episodes. It is **not** a causal decomposition: neither gap is statistically
significant, and the completed-episode restriction is itself a biased comparison. The
defensible claim is that the ITT crop-vs-pad gap must not be presented as a purely
behavioural difference while the arms are censored at materially different rates.

## 4. Scene-dependent uncertainty

Episodes are nested in scenes and share a map, so they are not independent draws. The
pinned 300 episodes span **8 scenes** (the full 1077-episode benchmark spans 11), with
per-scene episode counts from 9 to 84. Source: `tables/scene_uncertainty.csv`,
`tables/scene_per_scene_sr.csv`.

| arm | SR % | naive Wilson 95 % CI | naive width (pp) | scene-bootstrap 95 % CI | scene width (pp) | width ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stretchA | 15.00 | [11.40, 19.48] | 8.08 | [10.49, 19.91] | 9.42 | 1.17x |
| stretchB | 16.00 | [12.29, 20.57] | 8.29 | [8.60, 24.07] | 15.47 | 1.87x |
| crop | 16.67 | [12.88, 21.30] | 8.42 | [12.00, 19.92] | 7.92 | 0.94x |
| pad | 13.33 | [9.95, 17.65] | 7.70 | [5.75, 21.93] | 16.18 | 2.10x |

The scene bootstrap resamples **scenes** with replacement, carrying all of a drawn
scene's episodes, over 10 000 replicates.

- The effect is **large but not uniform**: pad 2.10x and stretchB 1.87x wider, while crop is 0.94x — i.e. slightly *narrower*. Acknowledging clustering does not simply inflate every interval; it makes the interval depend on how unevenly an arm's successes are distributed across scenes.
- Per-scene SR is highly heterogeneous (`tables/scene_per_scene_sr.csv`), which is what
  drives the inflation where it occurs.

Clustering diagnostics (**indicative only**):

| arm | ICC | design effect | effective n |
|---|---|---|---|
| stretchA | 0.0275 | 1.91 | 156.8 |
| stretchB | 0.1002 | 4.33 | 69.3 |
| crop | 0.0195 | 1.65 | 182.3 |
| pad | 0.2091 | 7.94 | 37.8 |

With only k = 8 clusters and cluster sizes spanning 9-84, the ICC / design-effect /
effective-n estimates carry very wide sampling uncertainty (two secondary height arms
even yield slightly negative method-of-moments ICCs). They should be reported as
indicative, and the directly observable quantity — the change in interval width when
scenes rather than episodes are resampled — should carry the argument.

Figure: `figures/scene_uncertainty.pdf`

## 5. Statistical resolution (minimum detectable difference)

Two-sided alpha = 0.05, power = 0.80, two independent arms with equal n, unpooled-variance
two-proportion z-test solved numerically. Source: `tables/mde_power.csv`.

| n per arm | minimum detectable difference (pp), p1 = 0.15 | p1 = 0.183 (full baseline) |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | 24.68 | 25.63 |
| 100 | 16.66 | 17.49 |
| 200 | 11.31 | 11.98 |
| 300 | 9.05 | 9.62 |
| 500 | 6.86 | 7.33 |
| 750 | 5.53 | 5.91 |
| 1000 | 4.74 | 5.09 |
| 1500 | 3.83 | 4.12 |
| 2000 | 3.30 | 3.55 |

- At the size actually used for the transform arms (**n = 300 per arm**), the smallest
  absolute SR difference detectable under these assumptions is **9.05 pp** (p1 = 0.15).
- At the height-sweep size (**n = 200 per arm**) it is **11.31 pp**.
- Every transform-arm contrast in this study is a few pp, i.e. well inside the region
  this design cannot resolve. The null results are therefore statements about
  **statistical resolution**, not evidence of equivalence.
- Detecting a 5 pp difference at p1 = 0.15 would require n = 906 per arm.
- Detecting a 2 pp difference would require n = 5274 per arm.

Terminology: this quantity is the *minimum detectable difference* / *statistical
resolution* under the stated assumptions.

Figure: `figures/mde_power.pdf`
