# ROBUSTNESS ANALYSES — sweep

Generated by `receipts/analysis/analyze_robustness.py` from the committed receipts.
These analyses were NOT pre-registered. They are reported because a reviewer will ask
for them, and because two of them materially qualify the headline numbers. Treat
everything here as exploratory.


## A. Run-to-run outcome instability (the non-determinism finding, quantified)

`stretchA` and `stretchB` are the SAME 300 episodes, the same settings, the same
driver, the same weights. Every difference below is pure run-to-run noise.

| | B success | B failure |
|---|---|---|
| **A success** | 30 | 15 |
| **A failure** | 18 | 237 |

- Aggregate SR is nearly identical: 15.0% vs 16.0%
- **Per-episode outcomes are not:** 33/300 = **11.0% discordant**
- Cohen's kappa = **0.580** (moderate agreement only)
- Of the 63 episodes that succeeded in *either* run, only 30 (**48%**) succeeded in *both*
- McNemar exact on the discordant pairs: p = 0.728 (no directional bias — as expected, since the two arms are the same condition)

**Why this matters for the paper.** The aggregate rate is reproducible; the per-episode
outcome is not. Roughly half of this benchmark's successes are coin-flips at the
episode level. This is the single most defensible statement of the non-determinism
finding, it rests on 300 episodes rather than the 6-episode probe, and it independently
justifies abandoning McNemar as the primary test (Amendment 1): a paired test on an
unstable pair measures noise.


## B. Differential compute censoring (a real confound — must be reported)

The runner kills every episode at a FIXED **900 s wall-clock** and records it as a
failure (`term_reason=wall_timeout`, SR and SPL forced to 0). This is a *compute*
limit, not a navigation outcome. If arms render at different speeds, they hit the wall
at different rates, and SR is penalised for being slow rather than for being lost.

| arm | n | wall_timeout | rate | ITT SR % | per-protocol SR % | shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stretchA | 300 | 20 | 6.7% | 15.0 | 16.1 | +1.1 pp |
| stretchB | 300 | 22 | 7.3% | 16.0 | 17.3 | +1.3 pp |
| crop | 300 | 6 | 2.0% | 16.7 | 17.0 | +0.3 pp |
| pad | 300 | 37 | 12.3% | 13.3 | 15.2 | +1.9 pp |
| h060 | 200 | 10 | 5.0% | 11.5 | 12.1 | +0.6 pp |
| h078 | 200 | 13 | 6.5% | 12.5 | 13.4 | +0.9 pp |
| h095 | 200 | 9 | 4.5% | 18.5 | 19.4 | +0.9 pp |
| h110 | 200 | 6 | 3.0% | 13.0 | 13.4 | +0.4 pp |
| h130 | 200 | 15 | 7.5% | 15.0 | 16.2 | +1.2 pp |
| h150 | 200 | 13 | 6.5% | 14.5 | 15.5 | +1.0 pp |

**The rates are not equal.** crop 2.0% vs pad 12.3%: difference -10.3 pp, z = -4.91, Fisher p = 7e-07. That is far more
significant than any SR difference in the entire sweep.

**Sensitivity.** The crop-vs-pad SR gap is +3.3 pp under intention-to-treat but only +1.8 pp once timed-out episodes are dropped — i.e. roughly 46% of that gap is a compute artifact, not behaviour.
Neither gap is statistically significant, so no conclusion changes. But the ITT number
must not be presented as a pure behavioural difference.

**Caveat on the per-protocol column:** dropping timed-out episodes is itself biased —
slow episodes are plausibly the hard ones, so removing them inflates SR for every arm
(every shift above is positive). Report both columns and claim neither as the truth.
The clean fix is a compute budget defined in *simulation steps* rather than wall-clock;
`step_cap` already provides one, and a future sweep should rely on it alone.


## C. Reach vs. arrival recognition

OS = reached the goal radius at some point. SR = *stopped* there. The conditional
SR|OS therefore isolates arrival recognition from navigation.

| arm | OS % (reached) | SR % (stopped) | SR given reached | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stretchA | 29.0 | 15.0 | **51.7%** | [41.4, 61.9] |
| stretchB | 31.7 | 16.0 | **50.5%** | [40.6, 60.4] |
| crop | 24.0 | 16.7 | **69.4%** | [58.0, 78.9] |
| pad | 29.0 | 13.3 | **46.0%** | [35.9, 56.4] |
| h060 | 27.5 | 11.5 | **41.8%** | [29.7, 55.0] |
| h078 | 28.0 | 12.5 | **44.6%** | [32.4, 57.6] |
| h095 | 31.0 | 18.5 | **59.7%** | [47.3, 71.0] |
| h110 | 22.0 | 13.0 | **59.1%** | [44.4, 72.3] |
| h130 | 24.5 | 15.0 | **61.2%** | [47.2, 73.6] |
| h150 | 25.5 | 14.5 | **56.9%** | [43.3, 69.5] |

**This REFUTES a claim currently in `FINAL_RESULTS_full1077.md` and `SLIDE_FACTS.md`.**
Those documents state, from an n=10 pilot, that *crop reached the goal but never
stopped*, and conclude that peripheral field-of-view is required for arrival
recognition. At n=300 the effect runs the other way:

- crop converts **69.4%** of reaches into stops
- stretch (A+B pooled, n=182) converts **51.1%**
- difference +18.3 pp, Fisher p = 0.008

crop *reaches* less often (OS 24.0% vs 29.0/31.7%) but *recognises arrival better*
when it does. The pilot's direction did not replicate. The FOV-for-arrival-recognition
mechanism must be retracted from those two documents before the paper reuses it; the
honest n=300 statement is that no transform shows a significant SR difference, and the
reach/recognise split is exploratory and unpowered.

**Two problems this creates.**

1. *The pinned subset is not scene-representative.* Several scenes are absent
   entirely and the surviving mix is reweighted. `PRE_REGISTRATION.md` asserts the
   0-299 subset is representative on marginal SR/OS alone; that is true of the
   margins and false of the composition. Since per-scene SR ranges from 0% to over
   40%, the margins agreeing is partly coincidence.
2. *Episode-level CIs are anti-conservative.* Episodes within a scene share a map,
   so they are not independent draws. Below, a **cluster bootstrap over scenes**

episode-level and ignore scene clustering.


## E. Record-schema anomaly (small, but disclose it)

9 records across stretchA (3), stretchB (4), h078 (1), h130 (1) carry `max_episode_steps = -1` and
`ended_at_step = -1`. These come from the **runner-side** wall-timeout stub in
`run_powered_benchmark.sh`, not the in-eval SIGTERM handler, so two writers with
slightly different schemas both produced records in this dataset. Scoring is
unaffected (SR/SPL are 0 either way and the distance sentinel is handled identically),
but any analysis of `ended_at_step` must exclude the -1 rows. ANALYSIS.md §5 currently
includes them in the step means; with 9 rows out of 2,400 the effect is negligible,
and it is noted here rather than silently fixed.
